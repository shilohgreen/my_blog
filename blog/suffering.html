Suffering

At the start of June this year, I fell ill with some pretty bad food poisoning, and I was prescribed some medication. One of the drug prescribed to me was called Metoclopramide (brand name is Reglan) for nausea. Upon consuming this medication, I started experiencing some very strange and severe side effects.

I first began vomitting uncontrollably. Which honestly did not alarm me that much since I was down with food poisoning.

What was more concerning was the sudden intense feeling of doom that flooded my mind. It's hard to put the exact feeling into words, but I distinctly remember this voice in my head screaming at me, saying it would all go away if I just committed suicide. It was genuinely frightening and unbearable.

Interestingly, I found that people on reddit experience something similar:

This side effect is actually well documented, it appears around 1 - 2 percent of patients that are prescribed it, and apparently the side effects can be permanent if given high enough dosages for long periods of time.

Anyways, this feeling waxed and waned for around a week. I was not myself at all, and I barely slept and ate for the entire time. I was extremely irrational and I could not tell what was real and what was not.

Part of this irrational fear included my mind being permanently damaged, but luckily, the effects slowly waned over the course of 3 weeks.

Psychedelics

After my recovery, I took quite a large dose of psilocybin in the form of magic mushrooms mid July. I did it alone in my dorm room, and I didn't really measure exact the dose, which seems reckless in hindsight.

It was an extremely intense trip, nothing that I have ever experienced before in my life (and I have done psychedelics before). Midway through the trip I decided it was getting too much and I ended up walking around campus with my friend.

Climax

At the highest points of the trip I absolutely forgot everything, and I mean everything. I forgot who I was, where I was, what I was doing here in America. I can only describe the feeling as being reset to a new-born child, but I wouldn't describe the feeling as particularly blissful, it was more confusing than anything.

Strangely enough, I was still completely coherent at this point, I could still hold a normal conversation with my friend, but it felt like every higher abstraction in my mind had just disappeared. I recall my friend asking me if he could leave me alone, because he had class at 3, and distinctly remember being so confused by that statement. What was a class? Who are you? And what does 3 mean? These words didn't mean anything to me.

Absurdity

I dont think I can sum up all the thoughts that went through my mind during this 6 hour period, but one thing that was constant was this feeling, that I can only describe as existential confusion. I wouldn't say this experience gave me access to some kind of hidden knowledge, but I looked at things in a very different way.

I saw these abstract man-made concepts like banking, money and how they literally shape the way people are incentivized

at the very least Questioning the nature of reality does not seem like a productive endevour
