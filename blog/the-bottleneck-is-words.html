The Bottleneck is Words

This essay is about fostering empathy, love and understanding through technological advancement

Understanding one another

Breeding empathy and understanding one another is a pretty important part of the human experience. I’m sure most people (at least on some level) view relationships with other people as a source of meaning in their life.

It’s a universal idea that spoken language and in-person contact is the gold standard for creating this feeling of shared understanding and emotional intimacy. I don’t think anyone would prefer a long distance relationship (romantic, platonic) over something in person.

Text based communications like emails or messaging platforms are obviously very limited, so we create mediums like FaceTime or voice notes to allow a better connection over long distances. But in the end, nothing can replace speaking face to face.

Visual cues through eye contact, body language, coupled with the subtle rhythm of a real-time conversation, are something no screen can fully transmit.

In person communication is our current answer to breeding love and shared understanding

Problems with Words

But I’m going to put forth the argument that spoken language is horrible at conveying feelings and fostering emotional intimacy.

Current language systems are horribly inefficient at conveying data. I can think of three main reasons why this is the case, firstly

  1. Its difficult capture feelings into a words

    Words are meant to be representations of very abstract concepts. Think of words like love, God and sad. Entire books have been written on these subjects, but yet we use them in everyday life to describe how we feel, somehow expecting them to breed shared understanding.[1] Naturally this leads to:

  2. Everyone having different interpretations of words

    We can both agree a movie is sad, but the movie could evoke very different feelings in our body and mind. I could try and explain to you what my version of sad means, how it evokes a sense of loss within me. But even if you intellectually understand what I’m trying to convey, you wont know the subjective experience of what I mean, because

  3. The meaning of words are built on our own experiences

    My experience of loss (or any feeling for that matter) is unique to who I am because it has been built on my past experiences and conceptions about the world. It’s what humans mean when “no one really understands me”. In some sense, its really true. There are 90 billion neurons in our heads[2], which means in a very real, statistical sense, every human emotion that has ever been experienced is a thumbprint that will likely be never experienced again.

    I think there’s both a sense of beauty and loneliness in understanding that.

Alternatives & the future

We only think words are the best because its all we’ve known and its the only solution we have at the moment. I believe that technology will provide the next leap in shared understanding and empathy since the evolution of written and spoken language

Technological solutions seems cold, harsh and inhuman only bc current tech is even more inefficient than spoken language. As I’ve spoke about earlier, calling, email, texts, are all lower bandwidth than spoken words / signals, so naturally they feel stifling and inhumane

I believe that in the future we will discover new ways to bond with other human beings through technological progress. Technology in the future will increase this bandwidth by allowing us to bypass auditory lang by manifesting emotion and thought directly in the minds of other people.

This may some pretty insane, but there are organisations already working on this, see:

  • Merge Labs
  • Nudge
  • Sci Corp
  • Neuralink
  • Motif Neurotech

Endgame

I do think that this is the one advancement that will bring humanity together. Right now human will is scattered on stupid goals like warfare, when people are starving on the other side of the globe. This isn’t really because humans are naturally evil or stupid, but simply because we are a young species that lacks wisdom and understanding.

Once we all understand each other deeply, activities like war, murder and genocide will be much more unappealing to humanity as a whole. Every human can gain an instant understanding to another, at any point in time.

In the moment when I truly understand my enemy, understand him well enough to defeat him, then in that very moment I also love him. I think it’s impossible to really understand somebody, what they want, what they believe, and not love them the way they love themselves. And then, in that very moment when I love them… I destroy them.

— Ender Wiggin, Ender’s Game

In the scifi book Enders Game, the protagonist learns about his enemy through this very same technology. In understanding his enemy, he fully gains the means to destroy them, but also inherits a deep love and understanding of their nature. He ends up being tricked into destroying their home planet but spends the rest of his life sick with regret.

To understand something deeply also imbues us with control and the ability to destroy. I believe that this technology will make humanity powerful beyond measure. Just like in the book, we will gain the ability to harm one another through our profound understanding of one another. But inheriting the love and empathy from that same shared understanding will make us never want to.
